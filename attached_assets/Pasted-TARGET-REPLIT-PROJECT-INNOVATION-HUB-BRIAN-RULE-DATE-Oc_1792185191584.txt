3. BUSINESS CONTEXT
============================================================

Matrix Imaging is a print, mail, and digital communications
company with approximately 180 employees.

Innovation Hub is intended to support users across:

- Executive Leadership
- Sales
- Account Management / Customer Service
- Operations
- Project Management
- Information Technology
- Finance
- Compliance
- Human Resources

Many users will have limited technical knowledge.

The application must not require users to understand
software development, APIs, databases, architecture,
or complicated project governance simply to begin work.

The intended business outcomes include:

- Capture business ideas and opportunities
- Evaluate potential improvements
- Prioritize initiatives
- Create and execute projects
- Assign owners and resources
- Track project progress
- Provide executive portfolio visibility
- Connect related clients, organizations, and programs
- Integrate with Jira where appropriate

Not every user needs access to or visibility into every
capability.

The application should be intuitive for occasional users,
not just administrators and project managers.

4. CURRENT USABILITY CONCERNS
============================================================

The following are known concerns requiring investigation.

A. OVERLAPPING TERMINOLOGY

Examples:

- Ideas
- Initiatives
- Projects
- Programs
- Organizations
- Clients
- Portfolio
- Dashboard
- Product Backlog
- Parking Lot
- Kanban

These terms may have valid technical or business meanings,
but their distinctions are not obvious.

Determine:

- What each actually represents in the application
- Whether users need to understand the distinction
- Whether concepts overlap unnecessarily
- Whether terminology is consistent across screens
- Whether terminology reflects normal Matrix business usage
- Whether some concepts should be hidden from general users
- Whether simpler labels would improve understanding

Specifically investigate:

What is the difference between an Initiative and a Project?

When should someone create an Initiative versus directly
creating a Project?

What is a Program, and when would Matrix use one?

How does an Organization differ from a Client?

What is the difference between Portfolio and Dashboard?

What is the purpose of Kanban?

What is the Product Backlog?

Why should an ordinary business user see product-development
backlog information?

What does Parking Lot mean to a new user?

Do not assume these concepts must remain visible simply
because they already exist.

B. NAVIGATION COMPLEXITY

Evaluate the entire navigation structure.

Determine whether navigation reflects:

- How users naturally think about their work

OR

- How the underlying application/database was designed

Identify:

- Too many primary navigation items
- Redundant destinations
- Confusing groupings
- Administrative functions exposed to general users
- Screens that belong within other workflows
- Inconsistent navigation behavior
- Missing contextual navigation
- Unclear next actions
- Pages that duplicate information

C. PROJECT CREATION

This is a HIGH-PRIORITY area.

Evaluate the complete process for creating a new Project.

Questions to answer:

- Where would a new user naturally begin?
- Can a user create a Project directly?
- Must they first create an Initiative?
- When is Initiative promotion appropriate?
- Are required fields understandable?
- Are users asked for information too early?
- Are relationships to Clients, Organizations, and Programs
  clear?
- Is the distinction between Project Owner, Sponsor,
  Manager, and Resources understandable?
- After saving, is the next action obvious?
- Does the application guide users toward execution?
- Are there unnecessary steps or screens?

Identify the simplest reasonable project-creation workflow
without removing necessary governance.

D. INITIATIVE WORKFLOW

Evaluate:

Idea
→ Guided Interview
→ Initiative Brief
→ Review
→ Approval
→ Initiative
→ Project

Determine whether this workflow is intuitive.

Evaluate whether:

- Users understand why an Initiative exists
- Users understand when to promote an Initiative
- Approval and review requirements are clear
- Users know what to do after saving
- The application provides useful next actions
- Too many intermediate states are exposed

Do not modify the existing Guided Idea Interview convergence
logic or scoring during this assessment.

E. RELATIONSHIPS

Map the actual relationships between:

- Organizations
- Clients
- Programs
- Projects
- Initiatives
- Resources
- Assignments
- Jira projects/issues

Identify:

- Required relationships
- Optional relationships
- Parent/child structures
- Unnecessary dependencies
- Confusing selection screens
- Places where users must understand the data model

Recommend how these relationships should be presented
in ordinary business language.
